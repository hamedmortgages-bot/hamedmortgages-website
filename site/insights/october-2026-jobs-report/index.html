Insights · Economy

Unemployment rose to 6.7% in October. Ontario is at 7.3%. What lenders will look at.

Hiring stalled and wage growth slowed. If your job or income feels less certain, the order in which you do things now matters.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerNovember 6, 20263 min read

What happened

Statistics Canada reported on November 6, 2026 that employment was little changed in October (down 4,700). The national unemployment rate rose 0.2 points to 6.7%. In Ontario it rose 0.3 points to 7.3%. Average hourly wages were up 2.2% from a year earlier, to $37.89, a little slower than September's 2.3%.

What it means for your mortgage

Your file, not the headline

Lenders do not look at the unemployment rate. They look at you: how long you have been with your employer, whether you are past probation, and whether your income is salary, hourly, bonus or commission. In a softer job market, they read those details more carefully.

Rates

The Bank of Canada weighs the job market alongside inflation when it sets its rate. Its next decision is December 9, 2026. Weaker jobs do not by themselves mean a rate cut, so plan with today's rates, not a guess.

If your income changes

A job change, reduced hours or a move to contract work can affect an approval you already have. Tell us before you close, not after. Lenders often re-confirm employment shortly before funding.

What to do now

  1. Buying: avoid changing jobs between approval and closing if you can. If you must, call us first.
  2. Renewing: if your income has dropped, compare the renewal offer with your current lender before you try to switch, because a switch usually means qualifying again.
  3. Worried about a layoff: build a cash cushion now and ask your lender what payment options your mortgage contract allows.
