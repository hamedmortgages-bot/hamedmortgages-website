Inflation stayed at 3.1% in October. What it means before the December 9 rate decision.

Gas kept prices high, while rent and shelter cooled. This is the last inflation number the Bank of Canada sees before its next decision.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerNovember 16, 20263 min read

What happened

Statistics Canada reported on November 16, 2026 that consumer prices rose 3.1% from a year earlier in October, the same pace as September. Gasoline was the main push: up 30.9% from a year ago, partly because prices fell sharply in October 2025. Without energy, inflation was 2.0%. Shelter costs rose 1.4%, and in Ontario rent rose 1.1%.

What it means for your mortgage

Variable rate

The Bank of Canada raised its rate to 2.50% on October 28 and said another increase may be needed. Inflation holding at 3.1% keeps that door open for December 9. If you are on a variable rate, plan for your payment possibly going up again, not down.

Fixed rate

Fixed rates follow bond yields, not the Bank's decision. Higher inflation tends to keep yields up, so fixed rates are unlikely to fall soon.

The good news under the headline

Without energy, prices rose 2.0%, right at the Bank's target, and housing costs are cooling. If oil prices ease, the pressure on rates eases with them.

What to do now

  1. Renewing in the next 6 months: ask for a rate hold now. A hold protects you if rates rise and you can still take a lower rate if they fall.
  2. On a variable rate: check what your payment would be at another 0.25% increase, so a December hike is not a surprise.
  3. Buying: get pre-approved with a rate hold before you shop, so your budget is locked.
