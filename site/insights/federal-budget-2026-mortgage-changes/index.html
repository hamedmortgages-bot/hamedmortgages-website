Budget 2026: the mortgage changes, and which ones are actually in force.

Higher insurance limits, 30-year amortizations, a longer foreign-buyer ban and new rules for multi-unit homes. Most are still proposals. Here is what to plan around and what to wait on.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerNovember 5, 20264 min read

What happened

The federal budget was tabled on November 5, 2026. For borrowers, the headline is mortgage insurance. Ottawa proposes to raise the home price limit for what the budget calls optional mortgage insurance from $1 million to $1.5 million, and to allow 30-year amortizations on those insured mortgages when at least one borrower is a first-time buyer or the home is newly built. It also proposes to extend the ban on foreign purchases of existing homes by two years, to January 1, 2029, with newly built homes exempt.

Other items: new mortgage insurance flexibilities for three- and four-unit homes, and private insurers allowed to cover five- to fifty-unit properties, both set for February 1, 2027. The Canada Revenue Agency would get $13 million over three years, starting in 2027-28, to build and pilot an income verification service for lenders and insurers. The government will also consult on clearer prepayment penalty information for insured mortgages, and plans regulations so the Bank of Canada publishes more information on insured mortgage rates.

What it changes for you

Proposed is not the same as law

The insurance limit, the 30-year option and the foreign-buyer extension are proposals. The budget gives no start date for the first two. Until the final rules are published, lenders and insurers keep applying today's rules to your file. Do not sign an offer that only works under the new limits.

Buying a new build or your first home

If the changes pass, a longer amortization lowers the monthly payment, but you pay more interest over the life of the mortgage. It can help you qualify; it does not make the home cheaper. Run both versions before you decide how much to stretch.

Building or buying a triplex or fourplex

The multi-unit changes have a date: February 1, 2027. If you are planning a three- or four-unit project, the financing options may look different after that date. Talk to us before you lock in a structure.

Self-employed and commission income

The CRA verification service is a pilot that starts funding in 2027-28. For now, nothing changes: your Notices of Assessment and tax returns are still how you prove income.

What to do now

  1. Buying under today's rules: get your pre-approval based on the current limits, not the proposed ones.
  2. Shopping above $1 million with 20% or more down: ask us to re-check your options once the final rules are out.
  3. Planning a multi-unit purchase or build: book a call before February 2027 so we can compare both sets of rules.
