The Bank of Canada raised its rate to 2.50%. What it means for your mortgage.

The first change in a year, and the Bank says another increase may be needed. Here is what to do if you are on a variable rate, renewing or buying.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerOctober 28, 20263 min read

Draft for review. Not yet approved by Hamed Ashouri.

What happened

On October 28, 2026 the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate by a quarter point, from 2.25% to 2.50%. It is the first change since October 2025. The Bank said inflation has been near 3% since April, pushed up by energy prices, and it expects about 3.5% by the end of the year. It raised the rate to stop high energy costs from spreading to other prices, and said another increase could be needed. The next decision is on December 9, 2026.

What it means for your mortgage

On a variable rate

Your rate follows your lender's prime rate, and lenders usually move prime soon after the Bank does. If your payment changes with prime, expect it to go up. If your payment is fixed, more of it now goes to interest and less to the balance. Your lender will tell you what changes and when.

On a fixed rate, or choosing one

Fixed rates follow bond yields, not the Bank's announcement days. A rate you already locked does not change. If you close or renew in the coming months, a rate hold protects you if rates keep rising.

Qualifying

The stress test still applies to new mortgages and refinances at federally regulated lenders: your contract rate plus 2%, or 5.25%, whichever is higher. A higher contract rate can lower the amount you qualify for.

What to do now

  1. On a variable rate: check your lender's notice and work out your payment if prime rose by another quarter point.
  2. Renewing within 120 days: have a rate held now, then compare before you sign.
  3. Buying: refresh your pre-approval so the amount reflects today's rates, before December 9.
