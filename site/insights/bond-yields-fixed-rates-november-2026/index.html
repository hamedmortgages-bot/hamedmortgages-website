Five-year bond yields jumped this week. Fixed mortgage rates usually follow.

The yield that lenders use to price five-year fixed mortgages rose from 3.35% to 3.59% in four trading days. Here is what that means if you are renewing or buying.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerNovember 13, 20263 min read

What happened

Bank of Canada data shows the five-year Government of Canada benchmark bond yield at 3.35% on November 9, 2026. It rose to 3.46% on November 10, 3.53% on November 12 and 3.59% on November 13. That is an increase of about a quarter of a percentage point in one week.

What it means for your mortgage

Fixed rates

Lenders price five-year fixed mortgages off this yield plus their own margin. When the yield rises this fast, fixed-rate offers tend to go up within days, sometimes before the news reaches the headlines.

Variable rates

Variable rates do not follow bond yields. They follow your lender's prime rate, which moves with the Bank of Canada. The next decision is December 9, 2026.

Rate holds matter now

A rate hold locks today's rate for a set period, often 90 to 120 days depending on the lender. If rates keep rising, a hold taken this week can save real money.

What to do now

  1. Renewing within about four months: ask for a rate hold now, from your lender and from us to compare.
  2. Buying: get a pre-approval with a rate hold before you make an offer.
  3. Choosing fixed vs variable: do not decide from one week of news. Let us run both options on your numbers.
