Insights · Market

GTA sales fell 7.9% in October and prices are down 4.3% from last year. Who has the edge.

Fewer sales, fewer new listings and softer prices. Higher borrowing costs are part of the story, but lower prices are offsetting them for many buyers.

Hamed Ashouri, Mortgage BrokerNovember 4, 20263 min read

What happened

The Toronto Regional Real Estate Board reported on November 4, 2026 that 5,603 homes sold across the GTA in October, down 7.9% from October 2025. New listings fell 11.9% to 14,173. The MLS Home Price Index composite benchmark was down 4.3% from a year earlier, and the average selling price was $1,024,108, down 2.7%. On a seasonally adjusted basis, sales and new listings rose from September and prices edged up. TRREB said borrowing costs have risen with higher inflation, but lower prices have offset that for monthly payments.

What it changes for you

Buying

With prices below last year, many sellers are more open to conditions and closing dates that suit you. Use that: a financing condition and a home inspection condition are worth asking for. Your budget should be set by the payment you are comfortable with at today's rates, not by what the bank will approve.

Selling and buying

If you sell in a softer market, plan for a lower sale price than a year ago and check how that changes your down payment for the next home. If you buy first, ask us about bridge financing before you sign.

Refinancing or taking equity out

A lower price index can mean a lower appraisal. If you are counting on equity for renovations or debt consolidation, get a realistic value before you plan around it.

What to do now

  1. Buying: get a pre-approval with a rate hold, then negotiate with conditions.
  2. Selling and buying: line up both closings and any bridge loan before you list.
  3. Refinancing: ask us for a value check first, so the numbers you plan with are real.
